## Course Rationale

This course provides the diploma student with a first integrated exposure to basic electrical and electronics engineering. The syllabus retains all prescribed topics, but teaching emphasis should remain on fundamental concepts, component familiarity, basic measurement, simple circuit behavior, and application-oriented understanding.

Broad topics such as transformers, AC machines, DC machines, transistor applications, operational amplifiers, and digital logic should be taught at diploma-entry level. Their treatment is to be limited mainly to:

1. identification of main parts,
2. basic working principle,
3. important characteristics at concept level, and
4. common practical applications.

Detailed design, advanced derivations, and device-level specialization are outside the scope of this course.

## Course Objectives

1. To build a clear understanding of basic electrical quantities, passive components, sources, electric and magnetic circuits, and AC fundamentals.
2. To introduce transformers and electrical machines through their parts, operating principle, and common applications.
3. To develop elementary understanding of semiconductor devices, diode and transistor action, and basic analog and digital building blocks.
4. To train students in the safe use of essential laboratory instruments for measurement, testing, and observation of electrical and electronic circuits.
5. To prepare students for later diploma subjects by connecting theory with simple practical work and everyday engineering applications.

## Reorganized Learning Sequence

The syllabus content remains unchanged in scope, but the teaching order below is optimized for first-year diploma learners. Basic electrical quantities are introduced before components, sources, and AC circuit analysis, and the laboratory sequence begins with safety and instrument handling before measurement and device experiments.

## Course Contents (Theory)

## Unit 1 - Basic Electrical Quantities, Components, and Sources

*(5 Teaching Hours)*

**1.1 Basic Electrical Quantities**

1.1.1 EMF, current, potential difference, power, and energy.  
1.1.2 Units and simple applications in circuits.

**1.2 Passive Components**

1.2.1 Concept and function of resistor, capacitor, and inductor.  
1.2.2 Typical symbols, units, basic characteristics, and common applications.

**1.3 Signal Waveforms**

1.3.1 DC and AC signals.  
1.3.2 Periodic and non-periodic waveforms.  
1.3.3 Elementary observation of waveform shape, amplitude, and time variation.

**1.4 Sources**

1.4.1 Ideal and practical voltage sources.  
1.4.2 Ideal and practical current sources.  
1.4.3 Source transformation.  
1.4.4 Simple numerical problems.

## Unit 2 - Magnetic Circuits and Electromagnetic Induction

*(7 Teaching Hours)*

**2.1 Magnetic Circuit Fundamentals**

2.1.1 M.M.F., magnetic flux, permeability, reluctance, and leakage factor.  
2.1.2 B-H curve and hysteresis loop.  
2.1.3 Hysteresis loss and eddy current loss.  
2.1.4 Analogy between electric and magnetic circuits.  
2.1.5 Simple concept-level problems.

**2.2 Electromagnetic Induction**

2.2.1 Faraday's laws and Lenz's law.  
2.2.2 Dynamically induced EMF.  
2.2.3 Statically induced EMF.  
2.2.4 Principle of self-inductance and mutual inductance.

**2.3 Direction Rules**

2.3.1 Fleming's Left-Hand Rule.  
2.3.2 Fleming's Right-Hand Rule.  
2.3.3 Simple application examples.

## Unit 3 - A.C. Circuits

*(10 Teaching Hours)*

**3.1 Basic AC Terms**

3.1.1 Angular speed, cycle, frequency, periodic time, and amplitude.  
3.1.2 RMS value and average value.  
3.1.3 Form factor and peak factor.  
3.1.4 Phase and phase difference.  
3.1.5 Impedance.

**3.2 Pure R, L, and C under Sinusoidal Excitation**

3.2.1 Voltage-current relation in pure resistance.  
3.2.2 Voltage-current relation in pure inductance.  
3.2.3 Voltage-current relation in pure capacitance.  
3.2.4 Phasor representation of each case.

**3.3 Simple AC Circuits**

3.3.1 R-L series circuit with sinusoidal excitation and phasor representation.  
3.3.2 R-C series circuit with sinusoidal excitation and phasor representation.  
3.3.3 R-L-C series circuit with sinusoidal excitation and phasor representation.  
3.3.4 R-L, R-C, and R-L-C parallel circuits at introductory level.  
3.3.5 Simple numerical problems only.

**3.4 AC Power**

3.4.1 Impedance triangle.  
3.4.2 Power factor.  
3.4.3 Active, reactive, and apparent power.  
3.4.4 Power triangle.  
3.4.5 Elementary numerical problems.

**3.5 Three-Phase Connection Basics**

3.5.1 Voltage and current relation in star connection.  
3.5.2 Voltage and current relation in delta connection.  
3.5.3 Simple problems only.  
3.5.4 No deduction required.

## Unit 4 - Transformer and Machines

*(6 Teaching Hours)*

**4.1 Single-Phase Transformer**

4.1.1 Main parts of a two-winding transformer.  
4.1.2 Basic working principle.  
4.1.3 EMF equation of transformer.  
4.1.4 Transformation ratio.  
4.1.5 Simple numerical problems.

**4.2 AC and DC Motors**

4.2.1 Identification of main parts of AC and DC motors.  
4.2.2 Basic working principle only.  
4.2.3 Common applications of AC and DC motors.  
4.2.4 Simple introductory problems and comparison at concept level.

## Unit 5 - Overview of Basic Semiconductor Devices

*(7 Teaching Hours)*

**5.1 Semiconductor Fundamentals**

5.1.1 Energy level diagram of insulator, conductor, and semiconductor.  
5.1.2 Intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductor.  
5.1.3 Importance of doping.

**5.2 P-Type and N-Type Semiconductor**

5.2.1 Formation of P-type semiconductor.  
5.2.2 Formation of N-type semiconductor.  
5.2.3 Majority and minority carriers.  
5.2.4 Basic properties and applications.

**5.3 PN Junction Diode**

5.3.1 Formation of PN junction.  
5.3.2 Forward bias and reverse bias.  
5.3.3 Forward and reverse characteristics at basic level.  
5.3.4 Common diode applications at introductory level.

**5.4 Transistor**

5.4.1 Concept of PNP and NPN transistor.  
5.4.2 Identification of emitter, base, and collector.  
5.4.3 Transistor operation in CE mode at concept level.  
5.4.4 Simple practical applications of transistor in CE mode.

## Unit 6 - Overview of Analog Circuits

*(5 Teaching Hours)*

**6.1 Op-Amp Fundamentals**

6.1.1 Features of an ideal op-amp.  
6.1.2 Pin configuration of 741C as a representative op-amp IC.  
6.1.3 Concept of virtual ground.  
6.1.4 Note: emphasis should be on general op-amp concept; 741C is to be treated as an illustrative example, not as the only modern device.

**6.2 Basic Op-Amp Configurations**

6.2.1 Inverting amplifier.  
6.2.2 Non-inverting amplifier.  
6.2.3 Voltage gain calculation of inverting and non-inverting amplifiers.  
6.2.4 Simple numerical problems only.

**6.3 Op-Amp Applications**

6.3.1 Adder using op-amp.  
6.3.2 Typical low-level signal conditioning applications.

## Unit 7 - Overview of Digital Electronics

*(5 Teaching Hours)*

**7.1 Logic Gates**

7.1.1 AND, OR, and NOT gates with truth tables.  
7.1.2 NAND, NOR, EX-OR, and EX-NOR gates with truth tables.  
7.1.3 Universal logic gates.

**7.2 Boolean Algebra**

7.2.1 Basic postulates of Boolean algebra.  
7.2.2 Simple algebraic simplification at introductory level.

**7.3 Standard Theorems and Forms**

7.3.1 De Morgan's theorem.  
7.3.2 Minterm and SOP representation.  
7.3.3 Maxterm and POS representation.  
7.3.4 Simple conversion and verification examples.

## Laboratory Syllabus

The laboratory component should support theory through observation, measurement, verification, and safe handling of instruments and components. The `2` laboratory classes per week may be organized as `15` sessions of `2` hours each.

## Practical Outcomes

After completing the laboratory course, the student will be able to:

1. Measure voltage, current, and power in a single-phase circuit with resistive load.
2. Visualize phase difference between voltage and current in series R-L and R-C circuits using an oscilloscope and draw the phasor diagram.
3. Measure voltage, current, power, and power factor in an R-L series circuit.
4. Identify different parts of a single-phase transformer and AC/DC motor.
5. Determine the transformation ratio of a single-phase transformer and measure its no-load current.
6. Identify active and passive electronic components in a given circuit.
7. Use a multimeter to measure the value of a resistor and verify it using color code.
8. Use an LCR-Q meter to measure the value of a capacitor and an inductor.
9. Test PN-junction diodes using a digital multimeter and obtain forward-bias V-I characteristics.
10. Identify the three terminals of a transistor using a digital multimeter.
11. Use an op-amp as inverting and non-inverting amplifier.
12. Realize truth tables of logic gates and verify De Morgan's theorem.

## Pedagogic Optimization Notes

1. All prescribed topics have been retained.
2. Greater teaching emphasis should be placed on fundamentals of electrical quantities, magnetic concepts, AC circuits, measurement, and component identification.
3. Transformer, AC motor, and DC motor coverage should be limited to main parts, working principle, typical performance idea, and practical applications.
4. Semiconductor, transistor, op-amp, and digital logic sections should be introductory and application-oriented, not device-design oriented.
5. Mathematical treatment should be limited to simple expressions and simple numerical problems appropriate to first-year diploma students.
6. Star-delta relations are to be taught without derivation, as already prescribed.
7. Laboratory instruction should begin with safety, polarity checking, instrument range selection, and correct connection practice.
8. Wherever possible, classroom teaching should connect the topic with home wiring, fan regulator, battery charger, motor drive, sensor interface, amplifier, and digital control examples familiar to diploma students.
9. Demonstration, simulation, and board work should be used together so that students with mixed academic background can follow the course more comfortably.
10. The reordered sequence should be followed as far as practical so that students meet prerequisites before formal analysis.

## Notes for the Teacher

1. This is a foundation course; depth should be sacrificed before clarity.
2. Students should leave the course with confidence in symbols, units, measurement, safe handling, simple calculations, and recognition of common components and devices.
3. The teacher may use modern instruments and examples, but the prescribed topics and syllabus boundaries should remain unchanged.
4. When discussing the 741C, the teacher may mention that modern op-amps are available, while keeping the prescribed syllabus content intact.
